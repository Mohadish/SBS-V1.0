    SBS Step Browser — User Manual

    1. Welcome to SBS Step Browser

    SBS turns a CAD assembly into a narrated, step-by-step illustration: every step is a complete picture of the scene — which parts are visible, where they are, what colour they wear, where the camera stands, what is drawn on top — and SBS animates the journey from one step to the next, speaks the narration, and renders the whole thing to video.

    What SBS is for

    Assembly and service instructions, training videos, exploded-view walkthroughs, sales demos of mechanical products. You load a model once, then author steps: hide a cover, move a screw out, point a camera, write a sentence. SBS handles the in-between motion, the voice, the subtitles, the header, the translation into other languages and the final MP4.

    The five ideas you need

    Project
    One .sbsproj file inside its own folder, plus sidecar folders for audio, render cache, media, languages, exports and backups. The project folder is meant to be moved or copied as a unit.
    Model and tree
    The CAD geometry (STEP, IGES, OBJ, GLB/glTF, STL) shown as a tree of folders and parts in the Tree tab. You can regroup, rename, archive, add hardware, cables, primitives, notes.
    Step
    A complete snapshot of the scene: visibility, transforms, colours, camera, overlay, narration. Steps are grouped into chapters. A step never depends on the steps around it — you can jump anywhere and the scene is correct.
    Transition
    What the viewer sees between two steps. SBS diffs the two snapshots and animates the difference according to the step's easing (Smooth / Linear / Instant / Instant fade) and an optional private animation.
    Export and cache
    Each step is rendered once into a per-step cache. When you edit something, only the steps it touches get a ★ star and are re-rendered. Everything else is stitched from cache.

    2. Getting started

    Install and launch

    Run SBS Step Browser Setup <version>.exe. The installer lets you choose the folder, creates a desktop and Start-menu shortcut, and does not touch your projects on uninstall. SBS is fully offline: the text-to-speech voices, the CAD converters and the video encoder ship inside the installer. Cloud text-to-speech and machine translation are optional and need an internet connection plus an API key.

    First launch is slower: the local voice engine warms up in the background (a few hundred megabytes of models are loaded once per session). You can start working immediately; narration playback becomes available when the status bar stops reporting the warm-up.

    Two projects at once. Launch SBS again while it is running and a second window opens (its title bar says (2)), and a third, up to eight — two projects side by side, as in 3ds Max. Each extra window works from its own copy of the settings: it starts the way the first window is set up, and what you change in it stays in it; the first window is the one that remembers. The licence is shared automatically. Copy in one window, paste in the other: overlay items (text boxes, pictures, shapes, tables, video clips) and table cells travel between SBS windows through the ordinary clipboard, look and all. A pasted item that uses a text style, shape style, constant title, pinned position or crop mask is pointed at this project's definition: the same brand definition when both projects wear the brand, else one of the same name, else the definition comes along and becomes this project's own (the brand notice will say so). Positions and sizes are in the export frame, never the screen: a copy from a 1280×720 project pasted into a 1920×1080 one is fitted by the ratio of the two frames, so it lands at the same place and takes the same share of the picture (text keeps its style's size, as with a brand). Copying from Word or Excel into SBS, and from SBS into them, works as before.

    Activating, renewing and moving your licence

    First launch. SBS shows Activate SBS with this computer's machine ID. Press 📋 Copy and send your distributor the machine ID together with your email address. They reply with a password and a long key; paste both and press 🔑 Activate. No internet connection is needed at any point — the key is checked on your own computer.

    Help ▸ Licence… shows who the copy is licensed to, the date it runs until and how many days are left, and the machine ID again with a copy button.

    • 🔑 Enter a new key… — renew early. You do not have to wait for the licence to run out: paste the new password and key whenever they arrive. Nothing changes unless the new key is valid, and if it would run out sooner than the licence you already have (an older key pasted by mistake) SBS says so and asks before replacing it.
    • Deactivate this computer… — takes the licence off this computer; SBS restarts and asks for a key. Save your project first: SBS refuses while there are unsaved changes, because an unlicensed copy cannot save.

    A key belongs to one computer. It is tied to that computer's machine ID, so deactivating does not move the licence somewhere else. To use SBS on a different computer — or after replacing a motherboard or a system disk, which changes the machine ID — install SBS there, copy the machine ID it shows, and ask your distributor for a key for it. Your old key is not destroyed: entered again on the original computer, it still works.

    A few days before the licence runs out SBS reminds you at start-up. After that date it shows License expired and waits for a new key; your projects are untouched.

    Your first project in ten minutes

    1. File ▸ New (Ctrl+N), then File ▸ Save As… to give the project a folder. Save early — the render cache, audio and backups live next to the project file.
    2. Open the Files tab and click Import. Pick a STEP, IGES, OBJ, GLB or STL file. STEP and IGES are tessellated on import; large assemblies take a while the first time and are then cached as fast-loading .sbsobj files.
    3. Look at the Tree tab. Rename or regroup folders so that they match how you want to explain the assembly (see chapter 4).
    4. Orbit with the left mouse button, pan with the right button or middle button, zoom with the wheel. Press F to fit the selection (or everything) in view.
    5. In the steps panel at the bottom, add a step. Hide a part (eye icon in the tree, or the Visibility commands in the right-click menu), move another with the gizmo, press C to capture the camera into the step. Add the next step and repeat.
    6. Type the narration for each step in the text field under the viewport. Press the 🔊 button to hear it.
    7. Press the ▶ button to play the sequence. Adjust transition easing per step if something should snap instead of glide.
    8. Open the Export tab, choose size, frame rate and format, and click Start Export. Steps are rendered once into the cache; later exports only re-render starred steps.

    What a project folder looks like

    Older projects. Projects saved before the folder layout existed keep their files next to the project (for example a _rendercache folder). SBS reads both layouts; new material is written to the new one.

    Saving, autosave, backups

    Ctrl+S saves, Ctrl+Shift+S saves as. Saves are atomic — the previous file is only replaced once the new one is completely written. Settings ▸ Auto-backup keeps timestamped copies in backups/; Back up now makes one immediately. Closing the window with unsaved changes asks you first.

    Rule of thumb. The project file is the truth. Anything you see in the viewport is rebuilt from it when a project loads, so a reload (Ctrl+R in the app window) is always a safe way to make the live scene match the data.

    Taking a project to another computer

    File ▸ Collect Project for Another Computer… packs everything the project uses into one .zip — like Archive in 3ds Max or Collect Files in After Effects: a copy of the project whose paths point inside the archive, the CAD models, every video clip the steps use (whole), the brand file, the interface library, and the project's audio/, languages/ and media/ folders. The render cache is a tick-box, off by default (it is large and the next export rebuilds it). Unzip anywhere and open the .sbsproj — nothing to relink.

    Where the pictures are. Overlay images, the logo and interface pictures are stored inside the project file itself, so they travel with it and never appear as separate files in the archive. Video clips are files and go to media/; the voice-over clips go with the audio folder the project names (audio/, or the custom folder an older project uses).

    The dialog lists every file with where it was found. Each one is copied into the archive, linked — kept out, the project keeps its absolute path (for a file on a restricted network share that cannot be read here: a computer that can see the share still loads it) — or skipped. A missing file can be located with Browse… first. COLLECT-REPORT.txt inside the archive says what was copied, linked and left out. Files are stored without compression (clips and models do not compress), so the archive is big — that is the point.

    Viewport basics

    • Orbit left-drag, pan right- or middle-drag, zoom wheel. Alt + wheel changes the perspective instead of the distance (see 6.4). F fits the selection; with nothing selected it fits everything. Cameras ▸ 🎯 Refit camera recomputes orbit distance and pivot after a model rescale.
    • Work camera (W): a scratch camera you can move freely without touching the step's saved camera. Press W again to return to the step camera. C captures the current view into the active step. While it is on, six standard views appear under the button — Top / Bottom, Left / Right, Front / Back, in the axis colours (X red, Y green, Z blue). Hover one to see, in the scene, the plane you would look through and an arrow along the direction you would look; click to switch there orthographically — the view fades out and back in (about 200 ms) rather than flying the camera across, and your framing is kept. Turning the view (Alt + middle-drag) leaves the standard view and brings back the perspective you had before it.
    • Select by clicking a part. Click again on the same spot to climb one level up the tree (part → folder → model). Double-click a locked folder to temporarily unlock it; it relocks when nothing inside is selected any more. Shift adds to the selection, Ctrl toggles, Alt inverts within the clicked unit. The cursor shows a small badge for the active modifier.
    • Gizmo appears on the selection: drag the arrows to move, the rings to rotate. L switches the gizmo between local and world axes. Type exact values in the numeric fields next to it.
    • Overlay edit (O) switches the mouse to the 2D layer on top of the viewport (see chapter 10). X toggles overlay x-ray so you can see the model through the overlay while editing.
    • Global mode (Space): while it is on, a transform you make carries into every step, not only the active one (see 5.5).
    • Esc cancels a placement tool or picker, closes a popup, or deselects.

    Narration bar and playback

    Below the viewport: ◀ ▶ step back and forward, ▶ plays the sequence from the active step, the text field holds the voice-over for this step, 🔊 speaks it. ← / → also move between steps when the focus is not in a text field.

    Steps panel

    Chapters are the grey headers; steps are the cards. A card shows its number, name, easing, and small buttons: 📷 update this step's camera from the current view, ✎ edit this step's private animation, ⧉ duplicate, 🗑 delete. A ★ means the step changed since it was last rendered; a chapter header shows how many of its steps are starred. Right-click anything for the full command list (chapter 7).

    4. Models and the tree

    Importing models

    Files ▸ Import accepts .step / .stp, .iges / .igs, .obj, .glb / .gltf, .stl and SBS's own .sbsobj. Several models can live in one project; each becomes a root node in the tree.

    • STEP / IGES are tessellated by the bundled converter. Settings ▸ CAD import sets the quality. After the first successful load SBS offers to save a .sbsobj next to the project — the exact same tessellation, loading in seconds instead of minutes. Accept it.
    • GLB / glTF / FBX / OBJ arrive with their own hierarchy and, sometimes, baked transforms. SBS flattens what it must and records each mesh's resting pose so that steps land on it exactly.
    • Units. Edit ▸ Model source transform… lets you scale, rotate or offset a whole model at the source level (Metric / Imperial helpers, per-axis reset). Do this once, before authoring steps.
    Keep the source files. The project stores the tree and identities, not the geometry. On load, SBS looks for the asset at the recorded path (absolute or relative to the project). If it is missing you get the asset check dialog: Browse… to relink, Override to load anyway with a placeholder, Clear All Warnings once you have fixed the paths. File ▸ Browse Assets (Ctrl+B) shows the same list any time.

    Reading the tree

    The Tree tab lists the hierarchy. Icons tell the node type: folder, mesh, 🔩 hardware, cable, primitive, flat shape, note. Left of every row: the eye (visible in this step), the lock (locked folders select as one unit and refuse edits inside). The filter bar narrows the list: Sel only selected objects, Arch only archived, HW only hardware, Vis only visible, Hid only hidden, Fold only containers (selected parts stay visible). Type in the search field to filter by name. Unhide all shows everything in the current step.

    Organising folders

    Visibility across steps

    The eye icon changes visibility for the active step only. To cascade, right-click ▸ 👁 Visibility:

    • ▶ 👁 Show on all following steps / ▶ 🚫 Hide on all following steps
    • ◀ 👁 Show on all previous steps / ◀ 🚫 Hide on all previous steps
    • ⦿ 👁 Show ONLY on this step

    Hiding a folder hides everything inside. A part that is hidden by an ancestor shows a dimmed eye. During a transition a part that becomes hidden fades out; one that appears fades in (the fade length is the visibility channel of the animation, chapter 9).

    Replace-models and following

    Two ways to say "this object should look different in some steps":

    • 🔄 Convert to Replace-Model makes the node a holder for alternative geometry. ＋ Add to replace… adds a variant; each step can show one of them. 🚫🔄 Remove from replace-model undoes it.
    • 🔗 Follow object… makes object A ride along with object B: wherever B's folder moves in later steps, A comes along, while A keeps its own visibility and colour. 🔗 Stop following… releases it. Following is the recommended way to attach hardware, labels or tools to a moving part.

    Notes on a part

    💬 Add Note… attaches a callout to the node; 🗒 Notes on this folder (n) lists them, with ↺ Reposition Note… and 🗑 Delete Note. See chapter 17.

    5. Selecting, moving, pivots

    Selection rules

    • Single click selects the smallest thing under the cursor (a part). Clicking the same spot again selects its folder, then the model — a climb up the tree. In the tree, click a row or Ctrl/Shift-click for multi-select.
    • A locked folder (🔒 in the tree) behaves as one object: clicking any member selects the whole folder. Double-click it to unlock temporarily and edit a part inside; it relocks by itself once nothing inside is selected.
    • Colors ▸ Select All Meshes / Deselect All do what they say. The Colors tab can also select every part wearing a colour.
    • Select tab: 💾 Save… stores the current selection as a named group; 📥 Load… re-selects it; 🔄 Update overwrites it from the current selection; 🗑 Delete removes it. Groups survive save/load and are handy for "all the screws of the cover".

    Moving with the gizmo

    The gizmo appears at the selection's pivot. Drag an axis arrow to move along it, a plane handle to move in a plane, a ring to rotate. L toggles local (the part's own axes) and world. The numeric fields accept exact values — type a number and press Enter. Every gizmo edit is undoable (Ctrl+Z). If the row's ✥ Move (or ⟳ Rotate) toggle is off, the stored position is not in effect and the part sits at its home position — dragging then starts from where the part actually is, and where you let go becomes the new stored position.

    What you move is stored in the active step only. Go to the next step and the part is back where that step says. That is by design: a step is a full snapshot. To move a part in many steps at once use Global mode or Group for global edit.

    Transform commands (right-click)

    Pivots

    Rotation happens around the pivot. ⊕ Copy Pivot / ⊕ Paste Pivot move a pivot between parts, and ⊕ Paste Pivot → selected steps (n) pushes it into a step selection. ⊕ Pivot Center via 3 Points… sets the pivot on the centre of a circle you click three times (hinges, shafts). 🧲 Snap Pivot to Surface… puts it on a clicked face. When a part rotates around a pivot between two steps, SBS animates the arc, not a straight line — also when the part sits inside a moving parent folder.

    Global mode (Space)

    Press Space to toggle Global mode (the HUD shows it). While it is on, a move or rotation you make is applied as a delta to the selected objects in every step the moment you deselect. Use it to fix an assembly-wide placement error without touching each step. Press Space again to go back to per-step editing.

    Group for global edit

    📦 Group for global edit… wraps scattered parts into a helper folder so that one transform can drive them together across all steps; it shows a dry-run of what it will change before doing it. Pair it with Global mode.

    Undo and the history tab

    Every edit — tree, transforms, colours, steps, overlay, cables, hardware, settings — goes through one undo stack. Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Y, or open the ↶ tab to see the list and jump back several entries at once. 🗑 Clear history frees memory on very long sessions. Loading a project clears the history.

    6. Colours and materials

    Colour presets

    The Colors tab holds a list of named presets. Every part wears exactly one preset per step; the preset is what steps store, not raw RGB. Change a preset and every part wearing it changes everywhere. + Add Color creates one; double-click to rename; the ★ Set as Default preset is the one new parts get.

    • Assign to Selected applies the highlighted preset to the selection in the active step (a visible colour change between two steps animates as a cross-fade).
    • ↩ Revert to Default restores each selected mesh to its default colour.
    • Highlight Color is the colour used for selection highlighting and for parts that a note points at.
    • The sort buttons float the colours used by currently visible objects (or by the selection) to the top; the rest turn grey but stay clickable.

    Material properties per preset

    PropertyEffect
    Solid colorThe base colour.
    OpacityClassic transparency (0–1). Transparent parts are drawn after solid ones.
    SolidnessX-ray strength. Below 1 the part becomes a ghost through which other parts show — this is the usual way to reveal an interior without hiding the cover.
    Metalness / RoughnessPhysically based shading: metals reflect the environment, rough surfaces spread the highlight.
    Reflection Intensity · Reflective (SSR contact reflections)Screen-space reflections for floors and polished plates. Cost is real; use on a few presets.
    Strip textures (pure solid color)Ignore the texture that came with the mesh and use the flat colour.
    Outline: Global default / Always on / Always offWhether parts with this preset get the silhouette outline from the Env tab.
    Flat mirrorTurns a planar face into a true mirror (console-driven feature; see chapter 21).

    Slider drags are batched into one undo entry per drag.

    Colours and steps

    A colour assignment is part of the step snapshot. To make a part red only in step 12, assign it in step 12. To make it red from step 12 on, assign it in step 12 and use the step's copy/duplicate to carry it forward, or assign it in each later step. Changing a preset's values (its RGB or solidness) affects every step and stars only the steps where a part wearing that preset is actually in frame (see 19.4).

    Deleting a preset is refused while a part still wears it, and the default preset cannot be deleted. Assign the parts elsewhere first.

    7. Steps and chapters

    What a step stores

    Creating and arranging steps

    • Add a step with the panel's add button: the new step starts as a copy of the active one, so you only change what differs. ⧉ on a card duplicates it in place.
    • Reorder by dragging cards; drag into another chapter to move it. Multi-select with Ctrl/Shift-click, then drag, copy or delete the whole selection.
    • Chapters group steps and give the header its chapter number and name. Right-click a chapter header for ✏ Rename…, 📋 Copy, 🗑 Delete, selection helpers and 📥 Paste steps into chapter.
    • Step groups: ⊞ Convert to step group turns a step into a container whose children play as one continuous clip (one narration, several motions). ⊟ Ungroup step reverses it.
    • Delete with 🗑, Delete or Backspace when the card has focus. The last step cannot be deleted.

    The transition row

    Under the active card sits the transition row: easing, camera mode (Free / Template), AL1 / AL2 durations and Smooth reparent jumps.

    EasingWhat the viewer sees when arriving at this step
    SmoothEases out of the previous step and into this one. Default.
    LinearConstant speed. Good for conveyor-like motions and camera slides that continue into the next step.
    InstantThe step arrives all at once: a purple INSTANT BLOCK is pinned to the top of the step's animation and holds every channel (camera, objects, colours, visibility, overlay, cables…). Use it for cuts.
    Instant fadeSame, but the picture dissolves to nothing and back over AL1 while it cuts.

    AL1 and AL2 are the two animation-length variables in milliseconds. Presets reference them instead of fixed numbers: AL1 is the default home of the camera channel, AL2 of the object, visibility and colour channels. Change them on the step to speed up or slow down that one transition; change the defaults in the Anim tab.

    Smooth reparent jumps: when an object changes folders between two steps but barely moves, hold it still instead of letting it swing through a large arc. The threshold is the distance its visual centre may move before the arc is animated normally.

    Instant on the first step of a chapter. Works like any other step. If an old project shows a strange half-transition on such a step, re-pick the easing once from the dropdown — that rewrites the instant block in the current format.

    Steps that are videos

    A step can also be a rendered clip imported from a render cache (marked "This step IS a rendered video") or contain a video overlay playing alongside the scene. Video steps are exported as-is and cannot be edited in 3D; edit the original project instead.

    Imported clip looks soft, blurry, pixelated, low quality — especially its last frame? The clip is the source project's cached segment, and here it is compressed a second time when this project exports. At the usual Medium (4 Mbps) that is fine for delivery but thin as a master, and a step whose motion runs straight into the next one (a short or zero hold) ends on a frame the encoder had no time to clean up. Fix, no code: in the source project set Export ▸ Quality to High — 8 Mbps or Very High — 16 Mbps, star the step (or type its number) and Re-render, then import it again. The final video here stays at whatever quality you export it at.

    8. Cameras

    Free cameras

    By default a step's camera is Free: whatever view you capture with C (or 📷 on the card) is saved in that step. Moving the viewport does not change the saved camera until you capture again. Press W to enter the work camera — a scratch view for inspecting the model — and W again to snap back to the step's saved camera.

    Camera templates

    The Cameras tab holds named views. + Save current view as template creates one from the viewport. For each template: ▶ Go To moves the live camera there (bindings untouched), 🔄 Update sets the template to the current view and binds the active step to it, 🔒 Lock protects it from accidental updates, ★ Set as Default makes it the camera new steps get, 🗑 Delete removes it (bound steps become Free with the last view baked in).

    Switch a step's camera mode to Template in the transition row and pick a name: from then on the step follows the template, and updating the template updates every step bound to it. Edit ▸ Apply Camera Template… binds a whole range of steps at once. 🎯 Refit camera recomputes distance and pivot to frame the entire scene — useful after a model rescale.

    Perspective — how wide the lens is

    Hold Alt over the viewport and turn the wheel: the lens opens up or flattens while the camera moves in or out to keep your framing. The subject stays exactly the size it was — only the amount of perspective changes. A badge at the cursor shows a box drawn at the current lens, so you can see the effect before you turn the wheel. Ctrl makes the step finer, Shift coarser, the same way they change the zoom wheel.

    Turn it all the way down and the view becomes orthographic: parallel edges stay parallel, like a technical drawing. Turn it up for a dramatic, wide-angle look. A wide lens stretches whatever sits near the edges of the frame — at the default setting a part in the corner is drawn about a third longer than the same part in the middle — so if an illustration looks distorted when the camera is close, this is the control that fixes it.

    The perspective belongs to the camera, so it is saved the same way the view is: press C (or 📷 Update step camera) to store it in the step, or 🔄 Update to store it in a camera template. Until you do, it is a live view like any orbit or zoom. Each template's list row shows its lens.

    How the camera animates

    Between two steps the camera interpolates position, target and zoom over AL1 (or over the camera channel of a private animation). With Instant easing it cuts. Orbit pivots and pull-outs stored on a step are honoured — except between two steps that hold the same view, where a pull-out would only rock the camera out and back over a shot that never moves, so it is ignored. The first step of such a run still arrives from somewhere else and keeps its own. When two steps hold different perspectives, the move is a dolly zoom: the lens and the distance change together so the subject keeps its size for the whole transition instead of swelling and settling at the end. The export renders exactly what the step camera sees at the export aspect ratio; the viewport may show a little more or less on the sides.

    Settings ▸ Camera holds the defaults for new projects (field of view, orbit behaviour, default background).

    9. Transitions and the animation editor

    Channels

    A transition is made of channels, each with its own duration. SBS animates whichever channels actually changed between the two steps:

    ChannelAnimates
    cameraCamera position, target, zoom (home: AL1).
    objObject moves and rotations, pivot arcs, reparent jumps (home: AL2).
    visibilityFade-in / fade-out of parts that appear or disappear (home: AL2).
    colorCross-fade between colour presets (home: AL2).
    overlay / overlaysThe 2D layer's fade-out of the old overlay and fade-in of the new one.
    insertHardware insertion (screws driving in, with the reposition pre-step).
    cableCable morphs and socket plug / unplug.
    shapeFlat shapes and primitives appearing, moving, resizing.
    notesNote callouts appearing and following their parts.
    narrationWhere the voice-over starts relative to the motion.
    pauseA time spacer with no channels — a deliberate hold inside a transition.

    Animation strings and presets

    The Anim tab shows the default preset as a string such as camera(AL1), color(500), visibility(AL2), obj(AL2). Channels listed together in one block run at the same time; blocks separated by a phase run one after the other. Numbers are milliseconds; AL1 / AL2 resolve at transition time from the step (or the project defaults).

    • + New Preset creates a preset; the ★ marks the Project default used by steps that have no private animation.
    • Presets can be imported from your personal collection (kept in user settings, across projects) and exported to it.
    • The three search zones filter the preset list by dragging channel chips into them; Clear all 3 search zones resets.

    Private animation per step

    Press ✎ on a card to open the editor for that step only. It shows the phases as blocks; drag channel chips between blocks to reorder what happens first, add an empty time block or a pause block, type a custom duration or pick AL1 / AL2, and remove blocks with 🗑 Remove time block. The raw string is shown underneath and can be edited by hand.

    Order of precedence. A step's private animation wins over the project default. The easing dropdown edits the private animation (it inserts or removes the instant block). Deleting the private animation returns the step to the default preset.

    Holds and narration timing

    After the transition the step is held while the narration plays. In the exported video the hold is the narration length plus Export ▸ Step hold (ms). A pause inside a transition is authored with the pause channel, not with the hold.

    🧲 The magnet. While you drag an item, its left edge, centre and right edge — and its top, middle and bottom — stick to the same lines of the other items, of the header items, and of the picture itself (its four edges and its centre). A thin guide shows what it is standing on — magenta for a line of another item, cyan for a line of the picture itself (an edge or its centre); two guides at once mean it sits on a vertical and a horizontal line. While you drag or resize, the floating panel that serves the item fades to a trace so it hides nothing. The part you hold is the part that sticks: hold an item by its left edge and the left edge snaps, hold it by the middle and its centre does; only when that line finds nothing nearby do the others get their turn. A selection of several items moves — and snaps — as one box, by its outer edges and its centre, so the group keeps its shape. Hold Alt while dragging to let go of the magnet for that moment (fine placement right next to a line). These are your settings on this machine, not the project's; nothing of the magnet is rendered or saved. 3D-anchored arrows are not snapped to (they follow the camera), and a masked picture counts by the window you can see.

    ✎ Lines and arrows: points, heads and bends. A line and an arrow are the same thing — a line is an arrow that wears no head. Click one and it is a unit: move it, turn it, scale it. Double-click it and you are editing its points: a dot appears on the head, on the tail and on every point between.

    • Drag a dot to move that point. The magnet works for points too: a point sticks to the other items, to the picture, and to the other points of its own line (so a bend lines up with an end) — never to another line's points. Shift keeps the point on one axis, Alt lets go of the magnet.
    • Double-click the line where there is no dot: a new point, right there.
    • Double-click a middle dot: that point is deleted.
    • Double-click an end dot: the arrowhead at that end goes on or off (a filled end dot wears a head). Both ends are independent — two heads, one, or none; give a line's end a head and it has become an arrow.
    • Drag the line itself and the whole thing moves, dots and all. Esc, a click on empty space or on another item puts the dots away.

    Right-click a line ▸ 〰 Bend chooses how it goes through its points — the cables' three ways: Corners, Smooth (Bézier) (a curve through every point) or Fillet (rounded corners), with a tight / medium / wide fillet — the cables' rule: the rounding starts the same distance before every corner, so a gentle turn gets a wide arc and a sharp one a tight arc (a corner that has no room takes less). New lines are born with Fillet, so the first point you add bends the cable way. Every point move, added or deleted point, head and bend is one undo step; linked lines carry their points, heads and bend to every copy. 3D-anchored arrows keep their own rules (their ends belong to the model).

    ⬠ Shapes into polygons. Double-click a rectangle, a triangle, a circle or an ellipse (or right-click it ▸ ⬠ Convert to polygon (editable points)): it becomes a polygon of free points and its dots appear at once — same place, turn, squish, colours, thickness, style, bond and pin, same place in the stacking order. From then on it is edited like a line: double-click it for its points (drag one, double-click an edge for a new one, double-click a point to delete it — a polygon keeps at least three), right-click ▸ 〰 Bend for corners, smooth or fillet. A rectangle's rounded corners become a fillet of the same size; a circle or an ellipse becomes eight points with Smooth, so it still looks round until you move one. The conversion is one undo step. A linked shape leaves its link (the other copies keep theirs).

    ⇧ Moving straight. While you are dragging — the mouse button still down — hold Shift: the move stays on one axis, straight along X or straight along Y, measured from where the item (or the group) stood when you pressed. Whichever way you have gone further decides, so you can swing from one axis to the other; let go of Shift and the item is free again. It answers at once, even with the mouse standing still, and it works with the magnet on or off (with it on, the magnet then pulls along the free axis only).

    Squishing shapes. Shapes squish the way they do in a drawing program: the shape changes, its outline stays as thick all round as its Thickness says. Squash a circle and it becomes an ellipse, squash a triangle and it stays squashed (it used to spring back to an equal-sided one), stretch a line or an arrow and its points move — the bends (fillet, smooth) are worked out again from them, so rounded corners stay round and the line stays even, already while you drag. The frame around several items always stands upright, even when the items in it are turned, so stretching it on one side squashes turned items along the frame — a turned rectangle becomes a slanted one (a parallelogram), a turned circle an ellipse turned its own way. Hold Shift (or use a corner on images) to resize in proportion instead — and hold Shift while resizing a circle or an ellipse to make it a perfect circle again, however squished it was. One resize or one rotation of a group is one undo step, and undo brings back everything.

    Crop masks

    A mask crops an image or video to a rectangle fixed on the canvas (a window through which the picture is seen). Right-click an image ▸ 🎭 Add mask… ▸ ▭ New rectangle mask… (private to this image) or 🌐 Use a shared mask… from the project library. ✎ Edit mask (moves every image using a shared one), ✂ Remove mask (show the whole image), 🎭 Use another mask…, 🌐 Set as global mask… publishes a private mask to the library.     Turning a mask. In the mask editor the rectangle has a ↻ knob above it and to its left — two, because the editor's own Apply/Cancel bar sits over the top one. Either turns the rectangle about its centre; hold Shift to land on 15°.

    The mask follows its picture. Move, turn or grow a picture and its own mask comes with it, so the framing you cut survives the edit instead of having to be cut again. Hold Ctrl to invert that for one gesture: the window stays exactly where it is while the picture moves under it.

    It follows position, rotation and even scaling — not squishing. Making a picture taller or wider is a deliberate distortion of the picture, so the window is left alone; growing it evenly is "bigger, same thing", and the window grows too. Ctrl is its own modifier layered over Shift: Shift keeps doing its job (snap the angle, keep the proportions) and Ctrl independently decides whether the mask comes along, so Ctrl+Shift gives you both.

    A note stays in the status bar for as long as the gesture lasts and changes as you press and release Ctrl, so it always says what is true right now. Everything is measured from where the gesture started, never accumulated, so Ctrl can be pressed or released halfway through and the mask lands exactly where it would have either way. One undo step covers the edit and the mask together. Only a private mask follows — a shared one belongs to every image using it.

The progress bar is not text, so the default style's colour does not reach it: its own Fill colour, Track colour and Track opacity sit in its editor, beside its position and size.

    Header tab

    Spotlight at this step

    "Before I insert the screw I want it up close to the camera." Right-click a folder, a screw, a primitive or a shape ▸ 🔦 Spotlight at this step. Orbit the camera on that step and it comes along; the next step puts it back where it lives — or, for a screw, the insertion animation takes it from there into its hole.

    Move or turn it with the gizmo and that place is kept. The white block beside the gizmo is the dolly: drag it up to send the object farther away (smaller on screen), down to bring it nearer (bigger) — always along the line from the camera through the object's centre, so it never shifts sideways. It looks like a scale; it is a distance. 🔦 Reset the spotlight place puts it back on the default; 🔦 Stop the spotlight at this step switches it off and the object is back where this step had it before: the spotlight is a layer over the step's own pose — absolute, but not destructive. Right-click the white block for the spotlight's own menu: reset, re-read the place from where the object is now, make this place the project default (every spotlight switched on from then starts there — saved with the project), stop.

    In the custom animation the move has a capsule of its own: 🔦 spotlight. Drag it into the sequence and the spotlighted objects (and everything inside them) travel in that slot, over its duration, instead of with 📦 obj — "camera first, then the screw comes forward, then the insertion". Without the capsule they move with everything else. A following object can be spotlighted too: on that step its pose is the picture's, on every other step it rides its target.

    The place is stored as fractions of the frame, not in millimetres or degrees of lens, so a different lens on the step or an export at another frame shows the same picture. The step's own saved camera is what counts: authoring against a view you have not saved is allowed, as everywhere in SBS, but the step will show the object from its camera — the status line reminds you to 📷 Update camera. A raw part has no transform of its own: put it in a folder (or Make transformable) and spotlight that. Global Mode never carries a spotlighted pose to other steps.

    Brand — the company standard in one file

    Tools ▸ Brand… manages a .sbsbrand file: the header items and logo, the header default, text styles, shape styles, constant-title positions, pinned positions and shared crop masks of a company. 💾 Save brand… writes it from a project that looks right (and links the project to it); on a linked project it offers to update the brand — the revision number goes up — or to save a new one.

    12. Narration, voices and subtitles

    Writing the narration

Pasting from a document is sanitised (smart quotes, invisible characters and paragraph breaks that upset the voice engine are normalised).     15. Cables and sockets

    A cable is a smooth tube between two sockets with a controllable path. Sockets are small blocks you attach to parts; cables plug into them. Both are animated between steps.

    Creating

    🔌 tab ▸ + New Cable: name, Cable color, Diameter (mm) (the tab's diameter field sets the default for new cables), socket W / H / D (mm) and Socket color.     Cables in steps

    Each step stores the cable's point positions and which socket each end is plugged into, so a cable can be unplugged in step 12, dangle in step 13 and be plugged into another socket in step 14.     16. Flat shapes and primitives

    Flat shapes (▰)

    A flat shape is a filled outline traced from a model face: highlight a gasket surface, mark a sealing area, show a footprint. Click the trace button, then a face — adjacent triangles within Crease angle (degrees) of the picked triangle's normal join the face set and their outline becomes the shape. Tighter = only flat regions; wider = catches gently curved surfaces. Shapes belong to shape groups (double-click to rename); the eye on a group toggles every instance in it; the lock makes the group select as one.

    • Edit re-opens the polygon in the viewport editor seeded at an existing instance. ✏ Edit shape… in the tree does the same.
    • Style: fill colour and opacity, outline colour and thickness — per shape, or from a shape style.
    • Textured plane: pick an image file, then click a face (or empty space) to drop a 2D plane sized to the image's aspect ratio — decals, labels, screens.
    • Shapes appear, move and fade with the shape channel and keep their end state exactly as the step says, even when the part under them fades out.

    Primitives (⬡)

    Boxes, cylinders, spheres and similar helpers built inside SBS, used as stand-ins (a missing bolt, a tool, a fluid volume) or as containers. Choose a type and size in the ⬡ tab, then click a face to place it tangent (Esc cancels), or 🎯 Align to surface… / Align by 3 points later. Primitives have a base face at the origin so they sit on surfaces naturally, and they can hold children like folders: drop parts inside and they move with it. Right-click a primitive: 📄 Paste (independent) versus 🔗 Paste Instance (linked parameters) decides whether a copy shares its dimensions with the original. Sizes and quality settings live in the project and are part of what the render cache fingerprints.

    How the render cache works

    The first export renders every step. From then on, Start Export opens a small prompt:

    • ★ Re-render starred — render the starred steps, reuse the rest. The normal choice.
    • Trust the stars — if the cache keys changed for a technical reason (an app update changed the fingerprint format) but nothing in your project did, adopt the existing clips instead of rendering. Use it once after such an update; if a clip then looks stale, star the step and re-render. When the render settings differ from the last render's (an update added a render option, you changed the background or the quality…) you are asked, with the list of what changed: OK reuses last time's clips and renders only the starred steps, Cancel renders everything. Only a change of size, frame rate or cache generation refuses on its own — clips of two sizes cannot be joined.
    • + current — also re-render the active step even if it is not starred.
    • ▶ Render full project (incremental) and 🎯 Re-render selection with a range like 0-10, C11, 124, 89-102 (C11 = chapter 11). With When finished, assemble the complete video unticked, the selection renders into the cache and nothing else is touched — segments outside it that are out of date are left alone and counted in the status line. Ticked, the whole video is assembled: out-of-date segments outside the selection render too, unless Trust the stars says to reuse them.
    • 🧹 Purge stale cache — delete clips no step refers to any more (old sizes, deleted steps). Never deletes clips that are in use.

    Stars: what changes what

    A step gets a ★ when something that affects its rendered frames changed since it was last rendered. SBS compares against the rendered version, so undoing the change removes the star again. The rules:

    You change…Stars…
    Visibility, a transform, a colour assignment, the camera, the overlay, narration text, hold, easing, private animation of step nStep n, and step n+1 (its transition starts from n).
    A colour preset's valuesEvery step where a part wearing that preset is in frame (see the frame scan below). Presets nobody wears in frame star nothing.
    A text style, shape style, linked shape, constant title, pinned position, crop maskEvery step whose overlay uses it.
    A camera templateEvery step bound to it.
    A cable's look (colour, diameter, style), a primitive's size or quality, a flat shapeEvery step where it is visible.
    Step order, chapter membershipThe steps whose predecessor changed.
    Animation preset (project default), AL1 / AL2 defaults, environment, export size, fpsAll steps.
    Header layout, subtitles, voice, narration speed, language textsNothing — composited at stitch time.
    Deliberately not starred. Editing a hardware template, a note template, or the geometry of a loaded model (relink, source transform) does not star steps automatically. After such edits, right-click ▸ 🔁 Re-render cache segment on the affected steps, or clear the cache.
    Cleared the star by mistake? ☆ Clear star only tells SBS to keep the current clip. If the clip looks wrong, right-click ▸ 🔁 Re-render cache segment.

    Scan steps for what is in frame

    Edit ▸ Scan steps for what is in frame… walks through every step with its own camera, at the export aspect ratio, and records which parts actually appear in the picture (ghosted and transparent parts count as visible). The record is saved next to the render cache and has two uses:

    • Stars become precise: changing a colour preset stars only steps where a part wearing it is really on screen, not merely un-hidden somewhere behind the camera.
    • Cache keys become narrower: a change to something off-screen no longer invalidates the step's clip.

    The scan takes a moment per step (it is a GPU pass) and reports progress in the status bar. Records are refreshed automatically for steps you leave after editing them and at the end of every export; run the menu command after large reorganisations or when you want the stars to settle before a big export.

    Exporting other languages

    Make the language active in Edit ▸ Languages…, then export. Each language has its own render cache folder, so switching languages does not throw away work; only steps whose overlay text differs are rendered per language.

    Pages

    • Build the pages — one per step is the starting point. A step with sub-steps counts as one unit (it plays as one clip); its sub-steps become extra lines on the same page. Hidden steps and hidden chapters are left out.
    • Selecting. A selection is always one unbroken range: click a step, then Shift-click or Ctrl-click the other end — everything in between is selected. There is no way to leave a hole in a range, so a join can never leave a step outside.
    • Joining. With a range selected press ⤵ Join steps 25–28 into one page above the list, or right-click the selection and pick the same command. The joined steps keep the animation's own numbers — 25, 26, 27, 28, not 25.1, 25.2 — because the manual and the animation must agree on what "step 27" is.
    • Splitting. Select one step inside a joined page: ✂ Start a new page at step 27 (button or right-click). Un-merge — one page per step undoes a join completely.
    • Leaving a step out. Click the 👁 at the end of a step's row (or right-click ▸ Leave … out of the document; with a range selected it applies to the whole range). The row stays in the list, dimmed, with a shut eye 🙈; click it again to put the step back. This is a document-only decision: the step is not hidden in the animation, the other steps keep their numbers, and a page whose steps are all left out is simply not printed.
    • The layout follows the number of steps. A page is on Automatic until you choose otherwise: join two steps and the page gets two pictures, three steps → three, four or more → four (sort the rest out by hand), always in step order; un-merge and the pages go back to one picture. Pick a template yourself and the page keeps it — choose Automatic again in the template list to hand it back.
    • Each page has a template: One picture — wide (16:9), One picture — tall (3:2), Two pictures side by side, Three pictures — one large, two below, Four pictures — two by two, or Large picture, short text. Templates are strict on purpose: a picture can only live in a template frame, so every page of the manual lines up.
    • A step that holds a video is pictured at one frame of that clip. The picture's bar then carries 🎞 Frame…: scrub the clip's window, step a frame at a time with ◀ ▶ or the arrow keys, or type the exact second. Each picture keeps its own frame, so two frames of the same clip can sit side by side on one page — put the same step into two frames and pick a different moment in each. Without a choice, a video step shows the first frame of its trimmed window, as before.

      Pictures are cropped into their frames. A frame does not need the video's proportions: the picture fills it and what does not fit is cropped. Click a picture to select it — then drag to move it behind its frame and use the mouse wheel to scale it about the pointer (one undo step per drag). The bar above the picture has Fill (back to the default crop), Whole (the entire picture inside the frame), − / + (5% a press) and a percentage you can type into — 100% fills the frame, and anything from 10% to 800% is allowed. The wheel moves in 2% steps; hold Ctrl for 0.5% and Shift for 10%. If you make the picture smaller than its frame the white page shows around it — that is allowed. Esc deselects.

    • Step numbers on the pictures. On a page that prints several steps every picture carries the number of the step it shows (the same number as its line), so the reader can match text and picture. Switch it off with Step number on each picture in the left pane.
    • Before and after. A step picture normally shows the step's end. When the end hides what happened — a part that is already inserted — add the before frame: Picture ▾ ▸ ↳ before step 27. It shows the state the step starts from (the previous step's end) seen through this step's camera, so the pair is framed identically; it carries no overlay or notes (those describe a step's end) and is labelled 27 before. Put the before-frame and the step's own picture side by side in a two-picture page.
    • Which picture. By default every frame is Automatic: the page's steps are shared out so that the last frame always shows the page's final state — four steps on a two-picture page show steps 3 and 4, on a four-picture page steps 1 to 4. Picture ▾ on the picture's bar — or the Picture 1 / 2 / … button of that frame in the left pane — opens a picker with thumbnails: every step of the page with its picture, number and name, under each one its ↳ before frame (tagged before; until it has been rendered the thumbnail is the previous step's, which shows the same state), and the current choice is marked. It lets you choose a specific step of the page, leave the frame empty, or take an external image — a photo or a drawing that is not part of the animation. An external image is scaled down to at most 2400 px and stored inside the project. A step picture is the step's export frame exactly as the video shows it — the step's recorded camera (even while Work Camera is on), 3D, overlay, notes and hardware tags — but without the animation's header items and subtitles (the page has its own header and its own text), and without selection highlights or other authoring marks. Step pictures are rendered when a page is shown or exported (the app walks the needed steps behind the workspace and returns to where you were); they are not stored in the project, so the file does not grow. A picture refreshes by itself when its step, its overlay, a colour or a style changes; after anything else (a reloaded model, render settings) use ↻ Render the pictures again in the left pane.
    • A step group lays itself out. A group is one instruction made of several sub-steps, and its page gets one picture for each sub-step that has a sentence — two, three or four frames, picked automatically — with every frame showing the state its own sentence describes and wearing that sentence's number (6.1, 6.2, 6.3…). A sub-step with nothing to say gets no picture, because it has no line to belong to; a group where nobody speaks keeps its single end picture. A group with more than four speaking sub-steps is split into parts of up to four, as evenly as they go (five becomes 3 + 2, seven becomes 4 + 3), each part on its own page, so every picture is shown — and the numbers simply carry on across the break. In the step list a later part appears as a row of its own: merge it into the page before it if you would rather have the whole group on one page, or hide it on its own. Hiding the group hides all of its parts. Used deliberately, groups become a way of arranging the manual: group the steps that belong on one page, and the page arranges itself. A layout you picked by hand is never changed; press Sync to bring an older document's automatic pages up to date.
    • Numbers in front of each line: the same step numbers as the animation (default), or 1, 2, 3 on every page, or none.
    • A step with nothing to say is not printed. A step that only moves the camera or slides a part into place has no text, and a numbered line followed by white paper helps nobody: it gets no line and no number, the lines below close up, and the contents leaves it out too. Its pictures stay — a silent step can still be worth seeing. With 1, 2, 3 numbering the count simply carries on; with the animation's own numbers the speaking steps keep them (so a manual may go 10, 12 — the same numbers the film shows). It is gone in the editor too, not just in print. To see the empty steps again, untick Leave out steps that have no text in the left pane: each one comes back as an ordinary numbered line — which is also where you type to give one a sentence. To leave a step out completely, pictures and all, use the eye in the step list.
    • Your own number on a line. Double-click a line's number badge and type what it should say — Enter keeps it, Esc puts it back. The picture that belongs to that step shows the same number, and so does the contents. It is a label for that one line only: nothing else is renumbered, and clearing it returns the line to the automatic count. In the editor a typed number wears an orange ring so it can be told from the automatic ones; the ring is never printed.

    Chapters and the table of contents

    Every page shows the name of its chapter at the top of its text, so a reader who opens the manual anywhere knows where he is. On the chapter's first page the name is a real heading — that is what the PDF's bookmark points at — and on the following pages it looks the same without creating more bookmarks: one bookmark per chapter, however many pages it has.

    Pictures of a step that shows an interface can be framed on the interface itself: tick 🎯 Frame the picture on the interface in the document settings and every such picture is zoomed so the panel fills its frame. Move or scale one yourself and your framing is kept from then on — switching the option off and on again never takes it back. The picture's bar then offers 🎯 Standard framing (follow the standard again) and ⊹ Set as standard, which makes the framing you just made the standard for every interface in the document — stored as the difference from the automatic framing, so it carries over to interfaces of other shapes and sizes.

    The document opens with a table of contents of its own (the video's time-code contents has no meaning on paper): one line per chapter that actually prints — chapter number, name, a dotted leader and the page the chapter starts on — and, under each chapter, a line per step: its number, its name and the page it is on, one step in and a shade lighter. Switch the step lines off with …and the steps under each chapter in the document settings; the chapter lines stay. It is built by itself and stays right by itself: join or split pages, leave steps out, sync with a changed animation — the page numbers follow. A chapter whose steps are all left out of the document has no line. In the PDF every line is a link to its page. In a Hebrew document it reads תוכן עניינים and runs right-to-left.

    • The contents is a real page and is counted: at the front it is page 1 and the first step page is page 2 (Page 2 / 41). It takes as many pages as its lines need (a chapter line is taller than a step line, and only the first page carries the title), and everything after it shifts accordingly.
    • Put it where it belongs. A contents is rarely the very first page. Drag its ⠿ grip in the list — a blue line shows where it will land — or right-click it: Move up / down, Move to the start / the end. It is anchored before a step, so it keeps its place when you join, split or re-sync pages. Everything is renumbered, and its own lines follow.
    • In the workspace it is a box of the list, 📑 Contents: click it to see it in the centre; the arrows reach it like any other page. The list shows the printed page numbers.
    • Switch it off with Table of contents (chapters → pages) in the left pane; the numbering then starts at 1. A project without chapters has no contents page.

    For now the contents lists chapters only. Listing the steps of each chapter as well is planned, once it is clear how much detail a long manual can take on one page.

    Custom pages

    Not every page of a manual is made of steps: a cover, a safety notice, a parts list, a revision table. ＋ Custom page (above the list) adds an empty page right after the page you are on. It carries the document's header, footer and watermark and is numbered like any other page; between the header and the footer it is yours.

    • The bar over the page adds items: ＋ Text and ＋ Picture ▾ — From a file… (stored inside the project, at most 2400 px; its frame starts in the picture's own proportion) or From the animation…: a list of every step, chapter by chapter, each with its thumbnail. A step picture is not stored: it refers to the step and is rendered like the page pictures, so it follows the step when the animation changes. With it selected the bar offers 🎞 Step N ▾ (another step) and ↳ Before (the state the step starts from, seen from its camera, instead of its final state).
    • ＋ Table adds a table — a parts list, a torque list, a specification.
      • Type: double-click a cell. Tab moves to the next cell, Shift+Tab back, Esc abandons that cell.
      • Pick cells: click one, or drag across several (Shift+click extends); click a grip to pick that whole row or column. Everything on the bar then acts on what you picked — add a row above or below it, copy the row, take a row or column away, or set the look of just those cells: bold, italic, alignment, text size, text colour and a shade.
      • Right-click a cell for the commands that belong to its row and column (add above / below / before / after, delete, merge); right-click a grip and the menu speaks only about that row, or only about that column.
      • Merge: pick a rectangle and press ⬓ Merge — the texts join into one cell. ⬚ Unmerge breaks it apart again.
      • Move a row or a column: drag the blue grip above a column or beside a row and drop it where the orange line shows. A row or column that runs through a merged cell cannot be moved — unmerge it first, and the app says so.
      • Sizes: drag the blue bar on a column border for its width, or on a row border for its height. A row you never touch is as tall as its text.
      • Paste from a spreadsheet: copy a block in Excel, click the cell it should start at (or double-click into it) and press Ctrl+V — tabs become columns, line breaks become rows, and the table grows to hold it. A single value lands in that one cell — and a picture on the clipboard lands inside the cell, scaled to fit it. If the copied block holds pictures in its cells, they come across with it: the spreadsheet also puts an HTML copy on the clipboard, and the pictures are read from it cell by cell. (A picture that floats over the sheet rather than sitting in a cell belongs to no cell, so it is not brought in — paste it into its cell on its own.)
      • Copy cells: pick a block and Ctrl+C. It goes to the clipboard as plain tab-separated text — paste it into Excel and it arrives as cells — and pasting it back into a table here brings each cell's look and picture with it, which plain text cannot carry.
      • Several rows or columns at once: pick them (drag across, or click one grip and Shift+click another) and the delete commands say so — Delete rows 2–4. ⧉ Col copies a column the way ⧉ Row copies a row, pictures and all.
      • The whole table still has its own Header row, Grid lines, Stripes, text size, alignment and colour. It keeps to its frame: make the frame taller (or the text smaller) if a row falls outside it.
    • Click an item to select it, drag it to move it, drag a handle to resize it; arrows nudge by 1 mm (Shift = 5 mm); Delete removes it; ⤒ Front brings it above the others. Items snap to a half-millimetre grid and stay inside the content area.
    • Double-click a text to type; click away to finish, Esc to abandon. Text wears the document's body font — the same one as the step texts, so the manual stays of a piece — and you choose the size (any number of points), bold, italic, alignment and colour. Hebrew and mixed lines are handled per box.
    • A picture is cropped by its frame like the step pictures: Fill, Whole, − / +.
    • Every gesture — a drag, a resize, a typed text, a delete — is one undo step.
    • The page has a name for the list (left pane), a ⠿ grip to move it anywhere in the document, and a right-click menu: move, rename, delete. It is anchored before a step, exactly like the contents.

    Tables, and a text box / table of this kind for the animation's overlay, are planned.

    Your own page templates

    When none of the prefab layouts fits, draw one. In the left pane, under the page's template, click 📐 New template…. The centre turns into a layout editor that starts from the current page's layout: the header and the footer are fixed grey bands, the text box is blue, the picture frames are green and numbered in the order the pictures fill them (the side panes are dimmed until you save or cancel).

    • Drag a box to move it; drag a corner or an edge handle to resize it.
    • Drag on empty paper to draw a new picture frame — or press ＋ Picture frame. Up to eight frames; a template with no frame at all (a text page) is allowed.
    • The bar shows the selected box's X, Y, W, H in millimetres — type exact values — and, for a picture frame, ratio buttons (16:9 3:2 4:3 1:1 3:4: the width stays, the height follows), ◀ # / # ▶ to change which picture the frame receives, and Delete frame.
    • Keyboard: arrows nudge by 1 mm (Shift = 5 mm), Tab goes to the next box, Delete removes the selected frame.
    • Everything snaps to a half-millimetre grid and stays between the header and the footer, inside the side margins. Boxes may not overlap: overlapping boxes turn red, the bar names them, and Save template waits until they are apart. A frame does not need the video's proportions — pictures are cropped into it.

    Save template asks for a name, stores the template inside the project, puts the current page on it and adds it to the template list of every page. A page that uses one of your templates offers ✎ Edit (changes every page that uses it) and Delete it (those pages go back to the automatic layout). In a right-to-left document your frames are mirrored like the prefab ones, so draw them in left-to-right order.

    Text

    Every line starts as the step's voiceover and follows it: change the narration and the document changes with it. To rewrite a line for print, click it on the page and type; clicking away commits it, Esc abandons the change. From that moment the line is the document's own copy (rewritten for the document, blue ring on its number) — the voiceover is not touched, and the other way round. If the voiceover changes later, the line gets an amber ring and the left pane shows ⚠ the voiceover changed after you rewrote this with the new voiceover underneath, so you can decide: keep mine or use the voiceover again. Hebrew and other right-to-left lines are laid out right-to-left automatically, number badge included.

    When a page has more text than its template has room for, the text area turns red with ✂ the text does not fit — live, while you type. Shorten the text, split the page, or choose a template with a larger text area. Export asks before it writes a PDF with a cut page.

    When the animation changes afterwards

    Pages refer to steps by their permanent identity, not by position, so you can keep working on the animation. When the step list no longer matches the pages, the ⟳ Sync button turns amber and steps the document does not know yet appear dimmed in the list. Syncing never changes anything silently — every adjustment leaves an ❗ on the page it touched, with a sentence saying what happened:

    What changed in the animationWhat sync does
    A new step between two steps of the same pageIt joins that page (➕).
    A new step anywhere elseIt gets a page of its own, in order (🆕).
    A step was deleted or hiddenIt leaves its page (➖). A page left with no steps stays, marked ∅, until you delete it.
    A step was moved to another placeIt leaves its page (↗) and is placed like a new step where it landed. A whole page that moved together simply moves, untouched.
    A picture was pinned to a step that left the pageThe picture is kept and flagged (🖼) — you decide.

    Read the marks, fix what needs fixing, then ✓ Seen — clear these marks (left pane) or ✓ Clear all ❗.

    Hebrew and other right-to-left documents

    Reading direction is always in the left pane, in the Document block under Step numbers — not only when the project is in Hebrew. It is Automatic by default: SBS counts the letters of everything the document prints, and when most of them are Hebrew or Arabic the whole document turns right-to-left — the header and footer mirror, lines and their number badges start from the right, picture numbers move to the top-right corner, and the first picture of a page is the right-hand one (the frames are mirrored across the page, so the reading order of the pictures matches the text). The built-in phrases print in the document's language (עמוד 1 מתוך 12, מהדורה B, לפני) unless you re-worded them. A mixed line — Hebrew with a part number or a torque in Latin letters — is handled per line. Force Left-to-right or Right-to-left if the guess is wrong for your document.

    Watermark

    The left pane has a 💧 Watermark section (click its title to open it). Tick Print a watermark on every page and choose the kind:

    • Text — DRAFT, CONFIDENTIAL, a customer name; two lines are fine, Hebrew too. Size is in points and takes any number; pick a colour. The default is a mid grey because it reads both on the white page and over a dark picture — black disappears on dark pictures, white on paper. In the PDF the mark is real, crisp text at any zoom.
    • Image — a logo or a stamp. Choose image… opens a conversion window, because logos arrive in three shapes and a watermark needs real transparency: artwork on a white background (the white is made transparent), artwork on a black background (the black is made transparent), or already transparent (kept as it is). SBS guesses which one it is and shows the result over a white page and over a dark picture so you can judge it; Make it one flat colour turns a coloured logo into, say, a grey mark. The conversion is exact — soft edges stay soft, colours stay true — and is done once: the converted image is what the project stores and what every output uses. Width is a percentage of the page width.

    Opacity and Angle are sliders that redraw the mark on the page while you drag (one undo step when you let go). Where: Over everything — the pictures too (the usual choice: the mark also protects the pictures) or Behind the text and the pictures. The watermark never gets in the way of editing: clicks go through it to the text and the pictures. It belongs to the document only — the animation and the video are not affected — and an image watermark is embedded once in the PDF however many pages there are.

    Designing the header and the footer

    Out of the box the header shows the logo (Header tab ▸ 🏷 + Logo; a project that never defined one shows its first visible header image), the company, the title and document no. · revision; the footer shows the project name, the chapter and Page n / N. To design them yourself, click the header or the footer on any page — or ✎ Edit… under Header and footer in the left pane. The page behind dims (it is only there to show the result; ← / → still walk the pages) and the band you are editing gets a dashed frame. The first time, the standard header and footer simply become movable items that look exactly the same — nothing changes until you change it. What you design is printed on every page: step pages, the contents, custom pages.

    • Header / Footer on the bar choose the band — or click an item of the other one.
    • Items work as on a custom page: click, drag, handles, arrows (1 mm, Shift 5 mm), Delete, ⤒ Front; text has size, bold, italic, alignment and colour and is centred vertically in its box. Items cannot leave their band (header 12–198 × 10–28 mm, footer 12–198 × 275–287 mm).
    • ＋ Field ▾ adds a value that fills itself in on every page: title, company, document no., revision, project name, chapter name, chapter number, page number, number of pages, date of the export. With a text selected the field is added to that text, so one box can read Page {page} / {pages}. On the page you see the values; double-click the text and you see what was written — the {names} — to edit it.
    • ＋ Picture ▾ adds the project's logo (it follows the project: change the logo there and it changes here; always shown whole) or a picture from a file (a certification mark, a second logo).
    • ▁ Line shows or hides the rule of the band.
    • On a right-to-left document the whole design is mirrored by itself — you design it once.
    • ✓ Done or Esc goes back to the page. ↺ Standard (or back to the standard one in the left pane) throws the design away; undo brings it back.

    The PDF

    Title, company, document number and revision (left pane) feed the header's fields. ⬇ Export PDF… writes the whole document (into the project's exports folder by default): real, selectable, searchable text — not a picture of text — with a bookmark per chapter.     Reload versus restart

    Ctrl+R reloads the app window and re-opens the current project from disk: the live scene is rebuilt from the saved data, which cures most "the picture looks wrong but the data is right" situations.     Memory

    23. Glossary

    AL1 / AL2
    Animation-length variables (ms) that presets reference; AL1 is the camera's home, AL2 the objects'.
    Archive
    Remove a part from every step without deleting it.
    Cascade
    The inheritance of visibility and transforms from folders to their contents inside a step.
    Constant (title / position)
    An overlay item defined once and shown at the same place on many steps.
    Fingerprint
    The hash of everything that influences a step's rendered frames; the render cache key.
    Frame record
    The list of parts that are actually inside a step's picture, produced by the frame scan.
    Global mode
    Transform edits carry into every step.
    Instant block
    A zero-length phase pinned to the top of a step's animation that holds every channel: a cut.
    Language pack
    The .sbslang.json sidecar holding one language's texts and voice choice.
    Pivot
    The point a part rotates around; also the centre of its animated arc.
    Preset (colour)
    A named material (colour, opacity, solidness, metalness, roughness) worn by parts.
    Replace-model
    A node that can show one of several alternative geometries per step.
    Segment / clip
    The cached render of one step (its transition and hold).
    Snapshot
    The complete scene state stored on a step.
    Solidness
    X-ray strength of a preset; below 1 the part becomes see-through.
    Star ★
    "Changed since last rendered" marker on a step; chapters sum their stars.
    Template (camera / hardware / note)
    A named definition that instances follow; edit once, apply everywhere.
    Work camera
    A scratch camera (W) that does not touch the step's saved view.
